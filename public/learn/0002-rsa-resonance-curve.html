RSA Resonance Curve

Lesson 2 · why HF amplitude isn't constant, and why that matters for RMSSD

Lesson 1 covered why LF/HF fails as a balance index. This lesson covers a different, more basic claim your simulator makes: that slowing your breathing to ~6 breaths/min raises RMSSD — and why a naive model would get that backwards.

1. What RSA is

Respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA) is the beat-to-beat speeding and slowing of heart rate with breathing — faster on inhale, slower on exhale. It shows up in an R-R interval trace as a wobble riding on top of the baseline, with an amplitude (call it A_HF) and a frequency equal to the breathing rate.

2. The naive assumption, and why it's backwards

If you model RSA as a fixed-size wobble — same amplitude no matter how fast or slow you breathe — then basic signal math says RMSSD (a successive-beat- difference measure) should scale with amplitude × frequency. Fix the amplitude, and slower breathing (lower frequency) means lower RMSSD. That's the opposite of what actually happens: paced breathing around 6 breaths/min is well known to raise HRV, not lower it.

3. The resolution: amplitude itself isn't constant

RSA amplitude follows a resonance curve that peaks around 0.1 Hz (~6 breaths/min) — the natural resonance frequency of the cardiorespiratory system, where the respiratory reflex and the baroreflex loop interact constructively.1 Individual resonance frequency varies (roughly 4.5–7 breaths/min1), but the shape is the same: amplitude grows sharply as breathing approaches that frequency, enough to outweigh the frequency term above and make RMSSD rise, not fall.

What your simulator actually computes src/sim/rrGenerator.ts — hfAmplitudeMs() — models this directly as a Gaussian resonance curve: amplitude floor 8 ms at the extremes, peak 60 ms at 0.1 Hz, width (σ) 0.095 Hz, scaled by vagal tone. The widget below is that exact curve.

"RMSSD proxy" here is a simplified teaching stand-in (amplitude × frequency), not the exact RMSSD formula — good enough to show direction, not a clinical number. Drag from 24 down toward 6 br/min: the naive constant- amplitude model's proxy falls the whole way. The resonance model's proxy instead rises to a broad peak inside the ~6–12 br/min zone, then eases back down slightly by 6 br/min — but even at 6, it's still higher than it was at 15. Non-monotonic beats monotonic-in-the-wrong-direction; don't read the exact peak location as "the" resonance frequency (see the callout above — individual resonance frequency varies).
